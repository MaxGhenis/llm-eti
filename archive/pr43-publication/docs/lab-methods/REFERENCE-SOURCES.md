PKNF design facts used: 16 decisions; reform after eight; five randomized sequences; 20 cents per task; tasks can be zero; maxima {14,16,20,21,22,24,25,30}; Prog taxes the entire income at 25% through 400 cents and 50% above it. Flat25 and Flat50 apply throughout. One decision is selected for actual transcription work/payment. Table 6 uses labor utilization, socioeconomic controls, and subject-clustered errors. Section 4.4's “extensive” response categories mean increase/decrease/no change, not the participation indicator. Footnote 7 and Figure 7 restrict bunching to endowments above 20, with ten decisions per subject. The precise permutation/matching algorithm is not established from these passages alone.

KW distinctions: equation 6 gives the limiting dominated width as elasticity approaches zero. Equations 7 and 9 use behavioral bunching, a counterfactual density, and (in equation 9) a friction correction. The separate bunching-hole lower-bound argument on printed pp. 682-683 is conditional on that model; it is not equation 12. Section II.A.4 invokes a continuous local limit to separate intensive and extensive responses. Equation 12 on printed p. 687 is an upper-bound approximation.

Kleven 2018 explicitly distinguishes the upper bound from approximations to the true elasticity. Writing r = Δz*/z* and d = Δt/(1-t), the displayed right sides are r²/d for KW equation 12, r²/(2d) for clarification equation 4, and r²/((2+r)d) for clarification equation 5. The latter underlies KW Tables II-III. The clarification assumes a small interior response and uses a trapezoid approximation. These formulas are not exact under the large PKNF notch.
